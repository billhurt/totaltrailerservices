Trailer servicing and fault diagnosis, priced up front

Two services available: a fixed-price general service, and fault diagnosis with component replacement for when something's already gone wrong. Both are carried out wherever your trailer is kept.

General service

The price is set by the trailer, not by how long the job takes. Count the axles, check whether it has brakes, and that's your price.

Braked or unbraked? Look at the front of the trailer. A braked trailer has an overrun coupling (the hitch slides back into a damper when you slow down), a handbrake lever and a thin steel breakaway cable. No lever and no cable usually means unbraked.

Fault diagnosis and component replacement

Quote on request

When something's already wrong, the price depends on what's causing it. Send a photo or a short video of the problem on WhatsApp and I'll give you an idea of what's involved before I come out.

Braking system faults

  • Trailer pulls to one side when you brake
  • Brakes binding, or a hub too hot to touch after a run
  • Trailer shunts into the car when you slow down
  • Handbrake won't hold the trailer on a slope
  • Brakes lock on when you try to reverse

Lighting and wiring faults

  • Indicators flash too fast, or one side doesn't work
  • Lights work on one car but not another
  • Lights flicker when you go over bumps
  • Corroded or bent pins in the plug
  • Brake lights stay on, or light up with the indicators

Component replacement

Once the fault's found, the worn or failed part can be replaced on the spot or on a return visit, depending on the part. That covers things like bearings, brake shoes and cables, lamps, plugs and wiring.

You'll get a price for the parts and labour before anything is fitted.

Before you book

The questions people usually ask first.

Do I need to bring the trailer anywhere?

No. The service is fully mobile: I come to the trailer. A driveway, yard or field entrance is fine, as long as there's room to get round the wheels. Firm, level ground is best.

How often should a trailer be serviced?

Once a year is a sensible minimum for most trailers. Get it done sooner if it tows long distances or heavy loads, or if it's been standing unused for months, when bearings and brakes are most likely to have seized or corroded.

Does the trailer need to be hitched to a car?

No. The trailer can stay where it's parked, but it helps if it isn't loaded and there's access to both sides.

Why mainly evenings and weekends?

I'm still serving full-time as an RAF Ground Equipment Engineer, so that's when I'm free. For most people that's also when the trailer isn't being used.
